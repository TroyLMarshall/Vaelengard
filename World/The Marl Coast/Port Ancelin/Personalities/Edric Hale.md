### Personality
- **Ideal:** "Sentiment doesn't fill a warehouse. Good arrangements do." (He doesn't think this makes him cold — he thinks it makes him honest about how the world works, and he's mildly baffled that people find it distasteful when he says it plainly.)
- **Bond:** The Hale name and its standing on Warehouse Row. He inherited a mid-tier factor's business and has spent thirty years trying to move it up a tier — this match with Iseult is, to him, simply the next logical step.
- **Flaw:** He genuinely doesn't understand why people find his framing of the courtship insulting. He'd never say it _to_ Iseult or Margery, but he says it freely to other factors, which is exactly how word gets around.
- **Mannerism:** Talks about people the way he talks about wool grades — appraisingly, efficiently, without malice but without much warmth either. He'd be a good witness for a guild dispute and a terrible one for a wedding toast.
### Voice & Dialogue Notes
Play him confident, businesslike, and _not_ aware he's the villain of anyone's story. He should never sneer or twirl a metaphorical mustache — the discomfort he creates should come from how reasonable he sounds while saying something faintly awful.

Sample lines:
- _(discussing the match with another factor, party overhears)_ "The Fleece & Anchor's reputation for discretion is worth more to this family than any dowry. Thomas has good instincts. He picked well."
- _(if confronted directly by the party)_ "I'm not sure what you're accusing me of. I want my son settled, established, and connected to a business that matters in this town. Is that not what every father wants?"
- _(if pushed on whether he cares if Thomas loves her)_ A genuine pause — not evasive, just recalibrating. "I assume he does. He's not a fool. But I'd be lying if I said that was the part I've been thinking about."
- _(about Iseult specifically, if he's being generous)_ "Sharp girl. Wasted behind that bar, if you ask me — though I understand that's not a popular opinion to have."
- _(if he learns the party has been investigating him)_ No anger — mild, almost approving interest. "Careful people. Good. I'd rather my son's match survive scrutiny than avoid it."
### Background Texture
- A mid-tier Warehouse Row factor — not struggling, not among the great fortunes either. Old money, in local terms, but modest old money, the kind that watches the great fortunes with quiet ambition rather than resentment.
- Has floated the Hale-Carrow match to at least two other factors as good for "consolidating trade relationships" — language that makes it sound like a merger, because to Edric, that's essentially what it is.
- Widowed some years back (useful detail if you want a reason he's leaned so hard into pragmatism — no one currently in the household to soften his framing of things, or push back on it the way a spouse might).
- Doesn't know Thomas overheard nothing of this — he assumes his son shares his read on the situation, or at least tolerates it, and would be genuinely startled to learn Thomas finds it uncomfortable.
### Running Him
He should never feel like an antagonist to _fight_ — he's an antagonist to **navigate**, and ideally one the party ends up feeling more complicated about than they expected:
- He's not lying to anyone. He's just saying the transactional part out loud, in rooms where Thomas isn't standing, because he doesn't see why he shouldn't.
- If the party confronts him, he doesn't get defensive — he gets curious, maybe faintly respects the nerve. He's a man who values people who do their homework, even when their homework is about him.
- The real tension isn't Edric vs. the party — it's whether the party tells Thomas what his father's been saying, and how Thomas reacts to finding out his father sees the relationship in purely strategic terms even if Thomas himself doesn't.
- He genuinely likes Iseult, in his own appraising way — which somehow makes him more unsettling to talk to, not less. He'd be thrilled by the match succeeding and utterly unbothered if it collapsed, so long as Thomas didn't embarrass the family name in the process.
### Secrets & Hooks (GM Eyes)
- He's already quietly asked around about Margery's plans for the tavern's succession — not maliciously, just factor's due diligence, the same way he'd check a warehouse's roof before buying stock in it. If Margery or Iseult ever found out, it would land very badly, regardless of his intentions.
- He and Sir Walter's son Renier have had at least one conversation about "modernizing" trade arrangements — Edric is quietly sympathetic to Renier's instincts, which could make him a minor thread into the Hall's coming generational tension if you want to connect this plot to the wider town politics later.
- If the match with Iseult succeeds, Edric will treat her, from day one, as a full business asset to the family — which she may find either useful or infuriating, depending on how she's grown by that point.
- If it fails, he won't hold a grudge against Thomas — he'll simply start compiling a list of other prospects, with the same flat efficiency, which is its own kind of quietly devastating for a party that's grown to like Thomas.
### Quick GM Cheat Sheet
- **If treated with respect/directness:** engaged, even pleased — he responds well to people who don't waste his time.
- **If accused or moralized at:** genuinely puzzled more than defensive — he doesn't experience himself as doing anything wrong.
- **If the party tries to appeal to sentiment:** mostly lands nowhere. He's not moved by "but do you love him," he's moved by "here's what this costs you if it goes wrong."
