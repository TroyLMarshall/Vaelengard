**The Greywick Saltings** are a stretch of tidal flats and shallow lagoons along the coast, a half-day's sail (or a longer, marshier road) from Port Ancelin — flat, grey, wind-scoured country that looks like nothing until you understand what's being grown there isn't a crop but salt itself, evaporated patiently out of seawater one shallow pan at a time.

## **Geography**
A maze of shallow, diked pans cut into the marsh, flooded with seawater and left to evaporate under sun and wind across the warmer months — the entire landscape is artificial in the way Warehouse Row is artificial, a natural resource reshaped entirely around extracting one thing efficiently. Narrow raised paths between the pans are the only solid ground; a stranger wandering off them at the wrong tide can find themselves calf-deep in brine mud before they know it.

## **The Brine Wardens**
Salt-panning is skilled, patient work disguised as simple labor, and the Saltings' pan-families — collectively known as the **Brine Wardens** — guard that skill the way the Greenshaw's shipwrights guard timber-judging. Reading a pan's evaporation correctly, knowing exactly when to rake and harvest before a rain ruins a season's work, is knowledge passed down rather than taught outright.
- The **Corbett family**, holders of the largest and oldest pan-rights in Greywick, function as something close to a local nobility — not through land in the usual sense, but through inherited control of the best-draining, most reliable pans, the salt equivalent of the Kestrels' prime grazing land in the Wold.
- Smaller pan-families work marginal flats much the way smallholders work marginal grazing — more exposed to a single bad season, more resentful of the Corbetts' comfortable position, and just as unlikely to say so openly.

## **Why Port Ancelin Needs Them**
Salt isn't glamorous, but the town would grind to a halt without it — preserving the fish catch, curing hides in Shearer's Ward's tanneries, salting meat down for winter, even the rough salt used in some of the Ward's dye and wash work. It's the kind of dependency nobody in Port Ancelin thinks about until a bad Greywick season or a Corbett price hike makes half the town's preservation work suddenly, uncomfortably expensive.
- Unlike wool, salt has no equivalent of the Tron — no town-licensed public weighing, no guild oversight from Port Ancelin's side. Whatever oversight exists is entirely Greywick's own affair, which means Port Ancelin has less leverage here than it does with almost any other trade good it depends on.

## **Culture & Temperament**
Isolated, insular, and quietly proud of being essential rather than admired — Saltings folk have a reputation (fair or not) for dry humor and long memories, the kind of community where an outsider is watched for years before being trusted with anything that matters. Marriages tend to happen within the Saltings or with a handful of other salt-producing coastal communities further off, rarely with Port Ancelin proper.

## **The Harvest Rhythm**
- **Spring–summer** — the working season, when sun and wind do the actual labor and the Brine Wardens do little but watch, rake, and wait, a patience that outsiders often mistake for idleness.
- **Harvest** — a narrow, weather-dependent window; a heavy rain at the wrong moment can ruin weeks of evaporation in a single afternoon, and pan-families who've read the season wrong can lose most of a year's income overnight.
- **Autumn–winter** — salt is carted or shipped out to buyers, Port Ancelin's factors among them, before the pans flood and go dormant until spring.

## **A Quiet Tension**
The Corbett family's grip on the best pans has started to chafe on smaller pan-families in exactly the way the Kestrels' grip on prime grazing chafes on the Wold's smallholders — and unlike the Wold, Greywick has no wool guild, no Tron, and no de Varnay Hall to eventually arbitrate the dispute if it boils over. Whatever happens in Greywick, Greywick settles itself, for better or worse.

## **Hooks**
- A poor salt harvest drives prices up sharply, and Warehouse Row's tanners and the town's fishmongers start quietly competing — or colluding — over what little reaches Port Ancelin's market.
- A Corbett heir, chafing against the family's isolation, tries to establish a direct relationship with Port Ancelin buyers that cuts out an old Greywick middleman — and makes enemies at home doing it.
- A smaller pan-family, ruined by a bad season and buried under debt to the Corbetts, looks to Port Ancelin for a way out — work, a loan, or something more desperate.
- Something's found preserved in Greywick brine that shouldn't be there — a body, a cargo, a secret someone hoped the salt would keep forever.
