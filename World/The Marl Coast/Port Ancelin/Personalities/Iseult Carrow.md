### Personality
- **Ideal:** "I decide who I am. Nobody gets a vote they didn't earn." (This is exactly why the town gossiping about her love life before she's made up her mind is grating her more than the courtship itself.)
- **Bond:** The Fleece & Anchor — not sentimentally, but practically. It's the one place she's genuinely good at something that matters, and the unspoken understanding that she might inherit a share of it someday is the closest thing she has to a life plan.
- **Flaw:** She conflates being handled with being cared for — anyone managing her feelings on her behalf, even with good intentions, gets the same sharp reaction as someone trying to manipulate her. She doesn't always distinguish between the two in the moment.
- **Mannerism:** Answers questions with a question when she's stalling, and drops the sharp tongue entirely — goes quiet and direct — when something actually matters to her. Regulars know the shift; the party might not yet.
### Background Texture
- Daughter of a wool factor's clerk — she grew up around trade talk and contracts, which is why she can drop a shrewd comment into a merchant's negotiation that changes its course. She knows the wool business better than most barmaids would, and better than most factors expect.
- She's the only person in the tavern who talks back to Margery without flinching — not out of disrespect, but because Margery seems to actually want that from her. It's possible Margery sees something of herself in Iseult, though neither would ever say so.
### Voice & Dialogue Notes
Play her quick, a little armored, genuinely funny when she lets herself be. She should never sound like she needs rescuing — if the party's instinct is to protect her from Thomas or from town gossip, let her push back on that instinct directly.

Sample lines:
- _(to the party, if she catches them asking around about Thomas)_ "Funny — I don't remember asking any of you to look into my business. Was there a meeting I missed?"
- _(on the town's gossip)_ "Half this town's decided how I feel before I have. It's a wonder anyone bothers asking me anything."
- _(on Thomas, if she trusts the party enough to be honest)_ "He's charming. I know what charming's worth — I've watched men charm coin out of factors twice their age. I'm not stupid about it. I just haven't decided if I mind."
- _(if the party's investigation genuinely helps her, rather than handling her)_ A pause, then, quieter: "...Thank you for telling me instead of deciding for me."
- _(if it goes badly — they meddled, or the truth was ugly)_ "I didn't need you to protect me from finding out. I needed you to just _tell_ me."
### Running Her Reaction to the Party's Involvement
This is the real engine of the session — Iseult's response to being investigated matters as much as what they find out about Thomas:
- **If the party is transparent with her early** (tells her they're looking into it, or lets her lead) → she's prickly but ultimately respects it, and may even use them as a sounding board.
- **If the party digs behind her back and she finds out** (Wat's bad at keeping secrets, or Margery lets it slip) → sharp confrontation. Not violent, not tearful — just precise and a little cold. She'll want to know exactly what they found and exactly why they thought it was their business.
- **If the party gives her a comfortable lie instead of a complicated truth** → she'll likely find out eventually (she's sharp), and the betrayal isn't about Thomas at all anymore — it's about being lied to by people she thought respected her.
- **If the party gives her the messy, honest truth** (Thomas is sincere _and_ his father's motives are transactional) → this is her best outcome. She's fully capable of holding both facts and making her own call.
### Secrets & Hooks (GM Eyes)
- She's already noticed Margery giving Thomas free drinks and has drawn her own conclusions about what that means — she just hasn't decided if she agrees with Margery's read yet.
- She keeps a mental ledger of trade gossip nearly as sharp as Margery's, though she'd never call it that. If the party earns her trust, she's a strong ongoing information source — arguably more useful long-term than Wat, since she filters what she shares rather than just repeating everything.
- If the courtship with Thomas eventually works out, there's a natural future hook: Iseult stepping more openly into the tavern's deal-broking role, possibly clashing with Margery's instinct to keep everything unspoken and off the books.
- If it falls apart, she doesn't become bitter or a plot device for Thomas's arc — she goes back to running the room, sharper for it, and probably has _opinions_ about the party's role in how it ended.
### Quick GM Cheat Sheet
- **If treated as capable and consulted:** warm (for her), quick-witted, genuinely helpful.
- **If treated as fragile or something to protect:** cold, precise, will call it out directly.
- **If lied to, even kindly:** this costs trust harder and longer than any other misstep in the session.
