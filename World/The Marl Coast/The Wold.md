**The Wold** is the belt of rolling hill country stretching inland from Port Ancelin — a day's walk, or a slow day's drive with a loaded flock — where the sheep are actually raised before any of the town's wealth ever reaches La Foire. If Port Ancelin is where wool becomes money, the Wold is where it's still just grass, weather, and livestock.

## **Geography**
Chalky, close-cropped downland rising gently from the coastal flat, cut through by a scatter of streams too small to name on any map but the shepherds' own. The soil is poor for anything but grazing, which is precisely why it's sheep country and not farmland — the Wold's families would tell you, with some pride, that they turn ground no one else wants into the wealth the whole town lives on. Old drovers' paths — worn bare in places, grassed over in others — thread between the hills, all of them eventually funneling toward the single main road down into Shearer's Ward.

## **The Droving Road**
The road down from the Wold isn't much more than a wide track, but it's the town's lifeline every bit as much as Varnay Quay. Flocks are walked down over one or two days depending on the distance, moving slowest right before shearing season when the ewes are heavy with wool and the lambs still need minding. Drovers — often the same families year after year, sometimes the flockmaster's own sons — know every safe crossing, every spot where a flock can be watered, and every stretch of road where a tired lamb tends to wander off and get lost or taken by something worse than a hedge.

## **Settlements**
The Wold has no town of its own — nothing grander than a hamlet — because everything of consequence eventually gets driven, carted, or carried down to Port Ancelin anyway. A few clusters are worth naming:
- **Millbeck** — the largest hamlet, really just a mill, a small chapel-of-ease, and a cluster of cottages around a stream crossing. Functions as an informal gathering point before the big drives.
- **Ashcote** — smaller, poorer, mostly smallholders' cottages strung along a single lane. The kind of place a flockmaster's factor visits once a season and a tithe collector visits twice.
- **Kestrel Down** — not a settlement so much as a name for the high pasture and the manor house that overlooks it, seat of the Wold's most prominent flockmaster family (below).

## **The Flockmasters**
A handful of families hold most of the good grazing land outright — inherited, purchased, or in a few older cases granted generations back by a de Varnay ancestor currying loyalty from the hills. They run large flocks with hired shepherds and seasonal labor, sell in bulk, and have the leverage to negotiate directly with Warehouse Row factors rather than taking whatever La Foire offers on a given day.
- The **Kestrel family**, of Kestrel Down, are the wealthiest and most established — three generations of careful breeding have made their fleece some of the finest to come off the Wold, and they know it. Their relationship with Port Ancelin is comfortable bordering on complacent: they've never needed to charm the town, only sell to it.
- Other flockmaster names worth having on hand for future development: **Vane**, **Ordway**, and **Sowerby** — families with smaller holdings than the Kestrels but enough land and flock size to matter when the wool guild needs the "big farmers'" opinion on something.

## **The Smallholders**
Far more numerous, far less secure: families working a few dozen head on rented or marginal land, utterly dependent on a fair grading at La Foire since they have no reserve of wool or coin to absorb a bad season. This is the Wold's real fault line, and it's not new — the **Mallow Affair** happened precisely because smallholders' wool was the easiest to quietly downgrade without consequence, and the resentment from it hasn't fully faded. Smallholder families tend to know exactly which flockmasters "did well" during the years Mallow was grading, and who never faced a reckoning for it.
- **Robin Attwood**, the shearer whose suspicion unraveled the Mallow Affair, comes from smallholder stock in the Wold — his family still works land there, and his own local fame as a folk hero cuts differently at home than it does at the Fleece & Anchor. Some smallholder families are quietly proud of him; a few flockmaster households still consider him a troublemaker who aired the town's dirty laundry to foreign buyers.

## **Seasonal Rhythm**
- **Lambing** (late winter/early spring) — the Wold's most labor-intensive stretch, when smallholders and flockmasters alike are stretched thin and hired help is scarce and expensive.
- **Shearing season** (early summer) — the whole Wold empties toward Shearer's Ward as flocks are driven down in waves, timed and sometimes quietly negotiated so rival families' flocks don't clog the same stretch of road on the same day.
- **The long grazing months** (mid-summer through autumn) — the quiet season, when flocks fatten on the hills and the Wold's families have time for the small disputes — boundary lines, straying rams, water rights — that don't reach Port Ancelin's notice but matter enormously locally.
- **Winter** — flocks are brought to lower, sheltered pasture; a hard winter can devastate a smallholder's flock in a way a flockmaster's larger, better-provisioned herd can absorb, which is its own quiet source of resentment.

## **Relationship to the Town**
The Wold needs Port Ancelin's market, its Tron, and its foreign buyers — there's nowhere else to sell wool in quantity. But the town needs the Wold just as much and rarely says so out loud; La Foire without wool coming down that road is just an empty square. This mutual dependence is mostly comfortable, but it means every grievance the Wold has with the town — grading honesty, prices, the de Varnay dues that indirectly shape factors' margins — has real teeth behind it, even if hill folk rarely bring that leverage to bear directly.

## **A Quiet Tension**
Renier de Varnay's talk of "modernizing" harbour dues has started reaching the Wold secondhand, carried by factors and drovers, and it's landed differently there than in town — flockmasters worry about margins, smallholders worry that any new cost gets passed furthest down the chain, onto them. No one in the Wold has any formal say in Hall or guild decisions, which is exactly the kind of arrangement that could curdle into real trouble if handled carelessly by the next generation at Ancelin Hall.

## **Hooks**
- A boundary or water-rights dispute between a Kestrel-tier flockmaster and a smallholder family, brought to town for arbitration — a chance to explore Wold politics through people the party actually has to deal with.
- A smallholder family convinced their wool is _still_ being quietly downgraded, years after the Mallow Affair's reforms — true, false, or somewhere in between.
- A drover gone missing on the road down, with a flock and a season's wages at stake.
- Robin Attwood's family in the Wold, if the party ever wants to meet the person behind the folk legend rather than just hear about him.
