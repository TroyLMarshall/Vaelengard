### Personality
- **Ideal (or what's left of it):** "A man's worth what he does next, not what he did once." He wants this to be true. Whether the town agrees, or whether _he_ fully believes it on his worse days, is worth leaving genuinely open.
- **Bond:** Whatever dignity he has left, which he now locates entirely in doing honest, visible, unglamorous work — cart-loading isn't a punishment to him anymore so much as the only penance he gets to choose for himself.
- **Flaw:** Pride, oddly, survived the fall. He resents being pitied more than being scorned, and a well-meaning "it's alright, everyone makes mistakes" from a stranger can land worse than an old grudge from someone who actually lost money to him.
- **Mannerism:** Doesn't meet people's eyes when they first recognize his name, but recovers quickly — a practiced, weary steadiness, like a man who's had the same recognition happen to him a hundred times and has learned to survive the first ten seconds of it.
### Voice & Dialogue Notes
Play him plain-spoken, tired in a bone-deep rather than dramatic way, with flashes of the sharp eye that made him a good grader once — before it made him a good cheat. He shouldn't perform guilt for the party, but he also shouldn't dodge it if asked directly.

Sample lines:
- _(introducing himself, preemptively)_ "Hugh Mallow. Figured you'd hear it from someone eventually — easier if it's me."
- _(on the scandal, if asked plainly)_ "Twenty years grading fleece, and I told myself the smallholders wouldn't notice a season here or there. They noticed. I was wrong about a lot of things that year." _(No excuses offered unprompted — he'll elaborate only if pushed.)_
- _(on the larger flockmasters who were never implicated)_ A long pause. "You'll get more out of me about my own sins than theirs. I made my bed. I'm not in the business of naming who else should've shared it — wouldn't change anything, and I've got enough enemies without adding men who could still make my life harder."
- _(on Robin Attwood, the shearer who exposed him)_ "Good lad. Sharper than I gave him credit for at the time — that's on me, not him. I don't wish him ill. Never did, whatever people assume."
- _(if genuinely respected or given a fair shake by the party)_ Visibly caught off guard. "...Don't get much of that. Thank you."
- _(pride flaring, if pitied too openly)_ A little sharper than expected. "I don't need the sympathy. I need the work, and I've got it. Let's leave it there."
### Background Texture
- Senior wool grader at La Foire for nearly twenty years before the fall — the post he'd expected to hold until he was too old to stand at the Tron.
- Systematically downgraded smallholders' fleece while passing inferior wool from larger estate flocks as first quality, likely for years before Robin Attwood's suspicions unraveled it (see _The Mallow Affair_).
- Stripped of his position and fined heavily, but notably not imprisoned — the larger flockmasters who benefited (and likely paid him) were never formally implicated, a detail that still quietly rankles smallholders and, privately, rankles Hugh too, though he won't say so to just anyone.
- Now works as a cart-loader at Warehouse Row — the lowest rung of the trade he once oversaw from a position of real authority — and, per Varnay Quay's records, sometimes works the Quay itself, in full view of the harbour he once judged from above.
### Running Him
Hugh works well as a **flexible moral-texture NPC** — he can plug into several different threads without needing his own dedicated plot:
- If the party's investigating anything grading- or trade-related, he's a natural source of institutional memory — he knows how the old grading system worked, who benefited from it, and where the bodies (figuratively) are buried on Warehouse Row, even if he's reluctant to name names.
- He's a good mirror for any plot involving trust, reputation, or second chances — including, notably, Thomas Hale's courtship of Iseult, if you want a thematic echo: a man whose reputation precedes him unfairly or fairly, depending who's asked.
- Worth deciding privately whether he's actually reformed or just resigned — and letting the party's treatment of him nudge that answer one way or the other, rather than fixing it in advance. A DC 14 Insight check could reveal genuine remorse, but even that shouldn't be presented as definitive proof of anything.
- He won't volunteer information about the flockmasters who profited alongside him unless there's real trust built first (start requests at DC 15, easing with rapport) — he's protecting himself as much as anyone, and he's learned the hard way what happens when the wrong information reaches the wrong ears.
### Secrets & Hooks (GM Eyes)
- He knows, or strongly suspects, at least one specific flockmaster family who paid him to look the other way — never proven, never pursued. This is a live thread if you ever want to revisit the Mallow Affair's unfinished business.
- He and Robin Attwood have never spoken since the scandal broke — not out of animosity on either side, as far as anyone can tell, just an unresolved awkwardness neither has found a way past. A quiet, low-stakes reconciliation scene (or a scene where the party accidentally forces one) could be a nice small-scale hook.
- Some smallholders still refuse to let him load their carts on principle; most simply don't think about it anymore. Worth using to show how a scandal fades unevenly across a town rather than uniformly.
- If the party ever needs someone who won't be missed or questioned much, doing quiet, unglamorous investigative legwork around Warehouse Row or the Quay, Hugh is a plausible, slightly sad choice — he's used to being invisible now.
### Quick GM Cheat Sheet
- **If treated with basic respect:** guarded but responsive, useful institutional memory.
- **If pitied openly:** prickly, pride flares, conversation shortens.
- **If accused or pressed aggressively on the flockmasters:** shuts down — self-preservation instinct is still strong, whatever else has changed in him.
