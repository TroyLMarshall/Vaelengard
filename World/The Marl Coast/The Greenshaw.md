**The Greenshaw** is old forest country, a hard two or three days' travel from Port Ancelin along routes that avoid the Wold entirely — the two regions share a coastline's worth of trade with the town but otherwise have little to do with each other. Where the Wold is open chalk downland, the Greenshaw is dense, managed woodland: the source of every mast, hull plank, cart-frame, and warehouse beam Port Ancelin will ever need, since the town itself builds nothing from raw timber and never has.

## **Geography**
Old-growth stands mixed with generations of deliberately coppiced and managed woodland — the Greenshaw's people learned centuries ago that a forest cut carelessly stops being a forest, and their entire economy depends on trees that won't be ready to fell for another lifetime. A network of rivers and log-runs, rather than roads, is how timber actually moves — logs are floated downstream in great chained rafts to a handful of river-mouth mill towns, where Port Ancelin's timber factors do their buying.

## **The Shipwrights**
Timber alone isn't the real trade — it's timber _judged, shaped, and matched_ to purpose that Port Ancelin actually pays for, and that expertise belongs to the Greenshaw's shipwright families as much as any wool factor's grading eye belongs to him.
- **The Wrightmoot** — not a single guild but a loose council of master shipwright families who set standards for what counts as proper mast-timber, hull-timber, and lesser stock. A ship's mate who's sailed long enough can often name which Wrightmoot family's mark is burned into a particular mast, the way a wool buyer might recognize a factor's baling style.
- Apprenticeship in a shipwright family is close to inheritance — outsiders are occasionally taken on, but rarely rise past journeyman, since the real knowledge (which grain of wood bends without splitting, which stand has been quietly reserved for forty years for exactly this purpose) is treated as family property, not a trade to be taught freely.

## **Who Else Works There**
- **Fellers and haulers** — the Greenshaw's equivalent of dockhands, physical and dangerous work; a badly judged fell or a log-run gone wrong kills people every season, the forest's version of a storm at the Quay.
- **River-wardens** — regulate the log-runs and mill-town landings, much as the harbourmaster regulates berths, and are just as capable of playing favorites when timber is scarce and buyers are desperate.

## **The Trade with Port Ancelin**
Timber factors from Port Ancelin travel inland to buy rather than the reverse — the Greenshaw has no need of wool and little interest in the sea. What they want is coin, and increasingly, standing orders: a Wrightmoot family that agrees to season and reserve timber years in advance for a specific Port Ancelin shipbuilder is entering a relationship closer to marriage than a single sale, since timber cut too early or matched wrong can ruin a ship's hull a decade after the deal was struck.
- A damaged relationship here doesn't show up immediately — it shows up years later, in a badly-built ship that fails in a storm the Wrightmoot could have warned against, had anyone still been asking.

## **Culture & Temperament**
Slower, more patient, and by outsiders' reckoning almost stubbornly long-sighted — a Greenshaw family thinks in terms of which grandchild will finally fell a tree planted this year. This creates real friction with Port Ancelin's faster, season-to-season wool economy: a factor wanting a quick deal and a Wrightmoot elder unwilling to rush a forty-year-old plan talk past each other more often than either would like.

## **A Quiet Tension**
Renier de Varnay's ambitions have reached here too, though differently than the Wold — there's talk, still vague, of Port Ancelin wanting a standing timber contract locked in at fixed rates for years, which would suit the Hall's shipbuilding plans nicely and suit the Wrightmoot families very badly if wood prices rise the way older Greenshaw voices expect. The Wrightmoot has not yet given an answer, and its silence is making certain people in Port Ancelin nervous.

## **Hooks**
- A shipment of mast-timber arrives warped or wrongly cut — sabotage, a genuine mistake, or a Wrightmoot family quietly registering displeasure with how they've been treated.
- A young Greenshaw apprentice, denied advancement within their own family's shop, seeks a life in Port Ancelin instead — a stranger to the sea trying to make a place for themselves in a wool town.
- A river-warden is caught favoring one mill town's landings over another, echoing the Tron-bribery taboo at La Foire, but with no equivalent town-wide institution to catch or punish it.
- Renier's standing timber contract finally reaches the negotiating table, and the party gets pulled in as escorts, witnesses, or go-betweens for a deal that could shape Port Ancelin's shipbuilding for a generation.
