### Personality
- **Ideal:** "Feed people well and most of the rest sorts itself out." A simple philosophy, genuinely held — he's watched Margery's family navigate decades of tavern politics and has concluded that a good stew solves more problems than people give it credit for.
- **Bond:** The Fleece & Anchor's kitchen, and by extension Margery's family, who took him on as a favor to his old captain when the accident ended his sailing days. He's fiercely, quietly loyal — not showy about it, but he'd never work anywhere else.
- **Flaw:** He's genuinely half-checked-out of the common room's social currents. He's not being coy or discreet on purpose the way Margery is — he simply doesn't catch half of what's said, and has stopped straining to. This makes him a poor gossip source and, ironically, a more trusted one, since regulars know whatever they say near him mostly just evaporates.
- **Mannerism:** Talks with his hands full — stirring, chopping, plating — and tends to answer questions a beat late, having missed the first half over the clatter of his own kitchen.
### Voice & Dialogue Notes
Play him warm, a little gruff, entirely unbothered by tavern intrigue because he's genuinely more interested in whether the mutton's turned out right. He's not simple — he's just chosen, deliberately, to not be part of the information economy that runs the rest of the building.

Sample lines:
- _(if asked about Thomas Hale)_ "The factor's boy? Comes in, orders the stew, tips fair. Don't know what else you want from me — I'm back here, not out there."
- _(on his deafness, matter-of-fact, no self-pity)_ "Lost the hearing on this side near enough thirty years back. Cannon, rigging, take your pick. Means half this room's business passes right over me. Suits me fine."
- _(if pressed for gossip)_ "You want gossip, you want Wat. You want a decent bowl of stew, you're in the right place." _(genuinely redirects, not evasively — he means it)_
- _(rare moment of insight, if something did reach his good ear)_ "...Now that you mention it — did hear something, few nights back. Didn't think much of it at the time." _(A useful GM tool: he can supply a clue the party missed, but only the half he happened to catch, and only if something specifically reached his hearing side.)_
- _(about Margery's family, warmly)_ "Four generations I've watched come through that door and this one's got her mother's hands and her grandmother's stubbornness. Good stock."
### Background Texture
- Former ship's cook, lost a leg to a shipboard accident decades ago — worth deciding privately whether it was a rigging failure, a fire, or something else if you ever want to tell that story, but it doesn't need answering unless a player asks directly.
- Taken on by Margery's mother as a favor to his old captain — meaning his loyalty predates Margery's ownership and runs to the family itself, not any one generation.
- His mutton stew is a minor town institution — regulars credit it as "half the reason the tavern survives slow winters," which is a nice bit of color if the party ever needs a reason to be in the tavern during an off-season lull.
### Running Him
Old Pell's value is less as an information source and more as a **texture and pacing tool**:
- Use him for warmth and grounding scenes — a quiet kitchen conversation while the common room's political tension builds elsewhere works well as a breather beat.
- He's a good "reveal filter" — if you want the party to get a clue but not the whole picture, let it come from Pell: half-heard, out of context, offered without him realizing its importance. A DC 12 Insight or a good follow-up question can help the party press him for the rest of what he half-caught.
- He should never feel withholding. If he doesn't know something, it's because he genuinely didn't hear it, not because he's protecting anyone. That honesty is what makes him useful in contrast to Margery's deliberate discretion and Wat's eager oversharing — three different flavors of "keeps secrets" in the same building, none of them identical.
### Secrets & Hooks (GM Eyes)
- Because half the room's conversations "never reach him," he's occasionally sat within earshot of things Margery would rather not have witnessed at all — worth deciding on the fly if the party needs an unexpected witness to something in a later session.
- Long-term texture idea: a quiet running bit where Pell's hearing is slowly, slightly worsening, and Margery's started having Wat relay things to him more often — a small, human detail that costs nothing to include and adds warmth to the tavern's found-family feel if you want it.
- He'd make a good source of _old_ town history — thirty-plus years of sailing and cooking in Port Ancelin means he remembers things from before most current gossip started, useful if a plot ever needs a link to the town's past (an old storm, a prior harbourmaster, an older scandal than Mallow's).
### Quick GM Cheat Sheet
- **If asked for gossip:** genuinely redirects, not evasive — just not his department.
- **If something happened to reach his good ear:** offers it plainly, almost as an afterthought, without realizing its weight.
- **If threatened or the kitchen's disrespected:** the one thing that gets an edge out of him — protective of his space, sharp but brief.
