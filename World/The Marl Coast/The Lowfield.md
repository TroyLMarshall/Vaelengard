**The Lowfield** is the flat, fertile farmland lying inland and somewhat south of the Wold — real soil, thick and dark where the Wold's is thin chalk, given over almost entirely to grain rather than grazing. If the Wold is where Port Ancelin's wealth comes from, the Lowfield is where its bread does — a distinction the town rarely thinks about until a bad Lowfield harvest makes it impossible to ignore.

## **Geography**
Broad, gently rolling fields divided by hedgerows and old boundary ditches rather than the Wold's open unfenced pasture — a landscape of ownership made visible, since arable land this good has been fought over, inherited, and subdivided for generations in a way hill grazing never was. A slow, navigable river winds through the Lowfield's heart, the main route grain takes toward Port Ancelin when the roads turn to mud.

## **The Manors & the Grange System**
Lowfield land is worked under an older, more formal arrangement than the Wold's flockmaster holdings — a scatter of manor estates, each with tenant farmers working strips of land in exchange for rent, labor, or a share of the harvest, and each manor maintaining a **tithe barn**, a granary building where a portion of every harvest is stored against bad years, church obligation, and the lord's own use.
- The **Averlyn family**, holders of the largest Lowfield manor, are the closest thing the region has to the Kestrels of the Wold or the Corbetts of Greywick — old land, comfortable wealth, and a habit of treating their tenants' loyalty as simply the natural order of things rather than something that has to be earned.
- Tenant farmers occupy a position distinct from the Wold's smallholders — legally bound to their manor in ways a smallholder isn't bound to any flockmaster, which makes Lowfield grievances slower to surface but, when they do, considerably harder to walk away from.

## **Why Port Ancelin Needs Them**
The Wold's chalk downland is sheep country precisely because it's poor for anything else — Port Ancelin cannot feed itself from its own hinterland, and the town's bread, ale-grain, and fodder all move down the Lowfield's river on barges before ever reaching a market stall. This is the town's single least-examined dependency: nobody at La Foire haggles over grain the way they haggle over wool, because nobody imagines a season where the grain simply doesn't come.
- A bad Lowfield harvest doesn't just raise bread prices — it raises tension across every district at once, since a hungry Shearer's Ward and a hungry Merchant Quarter have very different capacities to absorb the cost.

## **Culture & Temperament**
More settled and hierarchical than either the Wold or Greywick — generations of manor life have bred a certain formal deference in the Lowfield's tenant culture, at least on the surface, along with a sharp collective memory for which lord's family was generous in a hard year and which wasn't. Church presence runs stronger here too; a Lowfield parish priest often carries more day-to-day authority over village life than St Ancelin's priest does in Port Ancelin proper, given how tightly the tithe barn system binds harvest, faith, and survival together.

## **The Growing Rhythm**
- **Spring sowing** — the year's gamble placed, weather-dependent and anxious.
- **Summer** — the growing season proper, when a bad run of rain or an early blight becomes visible long before harvest, giving the Lowfield weeks of dread or relief before the outcome is certain.
- **Harvest** (late summer into autumn) — the region's equivalent of shearing season, all hands committed at once, with the tithe barns filling as grain comes in from the fields.
- **Winter** — the barges slow as the river risks freezing or flooding, and Port Ancelin's grain reserves (such as they are) have to last on whatever was moved downriver before the season turned.

## **A Quiet Tension**
The Averlyn family's tithe barns give them enormous quiet leverage — in a bad year, they decide whose rent is forgiven, whose family eats from the reserve, and whose doesn't, a kind of power the Wold's flockmasters simply don't have over their smallholders. Whispers travel occasionally that certain lean years have been managed less generously than the Averlyns like it remembered, though no tenant has ever said so where the wrong ears might hear.

## **Hooks**
- A poor Lowfield harvest threatens Port Ancelin's winter bread supply, and the party finds itself drawn into a grain shortage that no wool guild or Hall ruling can actually fix.
- An Averlyn tithe-barn steward is suspected of skimming grain meant for lean-year relief — a Mallow Affair echo, but with hungrier, more immediate stakes.
- A Lowfield tenant family, bound to their manor in ways a Wold smallholder isn't, seeks a way to buy free of their obligations — and looks to Port Ancelin coin or connections to do it.
- Renier de Varnay, eyeing a standing grain contract the way he's eyeing timber, opens negotiations with the Averlyns that could shift the balance of who feeds Port Ancelin and on what terms.
